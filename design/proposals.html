Proposals

Purely visual ideas, drawn with the kit's own classes, only while they are proposals. A board is the proposal: there is no ROADMAP entry until the maintainer approves it.

Boards 01

UI-HOME-LIVE

Folders shows what is inside and what just changed

Desktop

Why. Folders is where everyone lands and every row looks the same: a generic icon, a count and a path. Nothing says what a folder holds or that something just arrived, so the app feels still while it works. The proposal adds a Just arrived strip (the three latest changes and the device they came from), lets photo and music folders show their own content on the tile, replaces the card's progress bar with a ring around the tile, and leaves the path to the folder detail, which already shows Local destination. It needs the originating device on replica history rows and up to four local thumbnails or covers per folder (the gallery and music derivatives already exist). The phone's Folders list takes the same strip, tile and ring.

Accept

Folders opens with a Just arrived strip of the three newest changes across selected folders (file, originating device, relative time), hidden when empty; each opens its file detail. A photo folder's tile is a 2×2 mosaic of up to four local thumbnails and a music folder's a stack of up to three local covers, falling back to the icon tile when none is local; nothing loads from the hub. Each card ends with a caption line for its latest change and no longer shows the path. While syncing, a ring around the tile fills with files checked and the nine-dot sits inside it; a conflict draws the ring in the warning colour; an indeterminate cycle draws no ring. The card stays one focusable row with the status in its accessible name. Only opacity and transform animate, reduced motion removes the entrance, and light, dark and 760 px layouts do not clip.

UI-MOB-HOME-LIVE

The phone's Folders shows what is inside and what just arrived

Phone · Fold

Why. The phone is where photos and music arrive, and its Folders list is the same icon, count and caption on every row, so a sync that brought new things looks like a sync that did nothing. This is the touch version of UI-HOME-LIVE, with the same three ideas: a Just arrived strip, tiles that show content (a 2×2 photo mosaic, a stack of up to three covers) and a ring around the tile while syncing. What differs from desktop: the strip is one row of 48 dp chips that scrolls sideways and cuts the third chip at the edge, so there is nothing to hover and no list of buttons; a phone row has no path to drop (it never shows one) and instead gains a third line for the latest change; the badge stays an icon on the phone, as SPEC says, and becomes text on the Fold, where the strip shows all three chips at once and the rail replaces the bottom tabs. It needs the originating device on replica history rows (DESK-REPLICA-AUTHOR) and up to four local thumbnails or covers per folder, which the phone keeps in app storage already.

Accept

On the phone, Folders opens with a Just arrived row of up to three 48 dp chips (file, originating device, relative time) that scrolls sideways and is hidden when empty; each chip opens its file detail. Selected rows keep the 12 dp gap and 48 dp touch targets and end in a third caption line with the folder's latest change; the path is never shown. A photo folder's tile is a 2×2 mosaic of up to four local thumbnails and a music folder's a stack of up to three local covers, falling back to the icon tile when none is local; nothing loads from the hub. While syncing, a ring around the tile fills with files checked and the nine-dot sits inside it; an indeterminate cycle draws no ring. Phone badges stay icons. On the Fold (700 dp and wider with the icon rail) the strip shows its three chips in a row, badges keep their text, and a conflict draws the ring in the warning colour. The row stays one touch target with its status in the accessible name, the strip scrolls by touch with no hover affordance, only opacity and transform animate and reduced motion removes the entrance. Light and dark hold, the Fold's two states are drawn above, and large text sizes wrap the third line instead of clipping it.

UI-DEVICES-TOPOLOGY

Devices shows the hub and who reports to it

Desktop

Why. Arca is a hub with replicas, but Devices is a flat list that never shows it. A small map puts the hub on top and each device under it, joined by a line that encodes how fresh its last report is: solid for a recent report, dashed for an older one, none when it has never reported. A line says when the hub last heard from a device, never that the device is synced: sync state still lives in the rows and in Folders. Selecting a node highlights its row below, so the map and the list stay one source. It needs no new data: reports already carry the hub's receipt time. The phone shows the same map above its list.

Accept

Devices opens with a Network map above Hub connection and Devices: the hub on top, one node per authorized device under it, each with icon, name, role tag and a pill that pairs icon, text and colour with the time of its last report. The line to a node is solid for a report within the last 5 minutes, dashed for an older one and absent when it has never reported; the 5-minute limit is a product choice to confirm. No label on the map says synced or up to date. A node is a button with aria-pressed: choosing it highlights the matching row and scrolls it into view, choosing it again or pressing Escape clears it; nothing animates. While the hub is unavailable no line is drawn and the nodes read as last known, as the offline list does today. Under 761 px the map stacks into one column with no lines and the list is unchanged; light and dark hold.

Each row's freshness is a caption line pairing icon, words and colour (Reported 2 min ago, Reported 5 h ago, No report yet). A legend line names the three line styles. Nothing on the map says synced or up to date, and phone rows still hide chip text. While the hub is unavailable no line is drawn, the hub row is dashed with an Unavailable chip and rows read as last known, as the offline list does today. Light, dark, large text sizes and a 480 dp width hold without clipping.

UI-GALLERY-MOMENTS

The gallery reads as days, not as a wall of months

Desktop

Why. Scrolling thousands of photos is one uniform wall: a trip, a birthday and a week of screenshots look the same, and the only date cue is the rail. Capture dates already indexed are enough to group each month by day with a readable heading, give a busy day a larger first photo, fold sparse days into one quiet row and bring back photos taken on this calendar day in earlier years. A Years / Months / Days control changes density. No places, no faces and nothing leaves the device; the only new data is a per-day count and an index of dates for On this day. The phone gallery takes the same grouping, with day headings in place of its month headings.

Accept

The gallery header offers Years, Months and Days, Months being the default; within a month, Days lists days newest first as "Weekday D Month · N photos"; a day with at least eight photos starts with a hero spanning two columns and two rows beside complete justified rows, and runs of consecutive days under four photos each share one quiet row headed by their date range; On this day appears above the first month only when earlier years hold photos for today's date and disappears otherwise; Years and Months show one representative photo with its count and open the same gallery at that period; the rail, hover selection, hover video and the viewer behave as today; the same grouping appears on the phone. Capture dates only: no location, people or object grouping.

UI-MOB-GALLERY-MOMENTS

The phone gallery reads as days too

Phone · Fold

Why. This is the phone and Fold counterpart of UI-GALLERY-MOMENTS: the same grouping, drawn for touch. Today the phone timeline is one uniform wall under month headings, with pinch levels of four (six on the Fold) columns, ten (twelve) columns and Years. The proposal turns the base level into days: each group has a readable heading and count, a busy day opens with a hero tile two columns wide, sparse days fold into one quiet row, and a strip of cards brings back the same calendar day from earlier years. The pinch levels become named: Days is the new base level, Months is today's dense grid with month headings, and Years stays as it is; a small pill names the level while a pinch is in progress and fades a second after, because there is no hover to discover it. What differs from the desktop board: a phone has no rail, so the existing fast-scroll thumb and its month bubble stay; the Fold puts On this day in a sticky side column, as SPEC does for two-column layouts, and offers the three levels as a segmented control in the header so they are reachable without a two-finger gesture. Only capture dates are used: no places and no faces, and nothing leaves the device. It needs a per-day count and an index of dates for On this day from the same hub gallery index the months already come from, falling back to the phone's own files offline.

Accept

On the phone and the Fold, the gallery's base level groups photos by capture day under a heading such as "Saturday 12 September" with its count; a day of eight or more photos opens with a hero tile spanning two columns and two rows (three by two on the Fold's six columns), followed by the rest in the usual square tiles with 4 dp gaps; consecutive days of fewer than four photos share one Quiet days group with its date range and count. Months is the existing ten- or twelve-column level with month headings and Years is unchanged; each inward pinch still advances one level and spreading reverses it, one level per gesture, anchored as today. While a pinch is in progress a pill names the level (Years, Months or Days) and fades one second after the gesture ends; accessibility exposes the three levels as actions. On this day shows up to three cards, newest year first, each with the year and the count of photos taken on today's calendar day in an earlier year; a card opens that day in the viewer's sequence, the strip is hidden when there are none, and on the Fold the cards sit in a side column that sticks while the timeline scrolls. The Fold header carries the Years / Months / Days segmented control. The fast-scroll thumb, its month bubble and year chips, selection by long press and the viewer are unchanged; day headings never move what is being viewed when rows load above the viewport. Dates come from the same capture date the months use, the day is the device's local day, the offline gallery builds the groups from the phone's own files, and no place or face data is read. Tiles keep the media placeholder token for unfetched photos, controls keep 48 dp targets, only transform and opacity animate and reduced motion removes the pill's fade; light, dark and 392 dp layouts do not clip.

UI-MOB-VIEWER-GESTURES

The photo viewer answers to the hand, not to buttons

Phone · Fold

Why. A phone photo viewer is the one place where people already expect gestures, and Arca's is a black page with a button bar and a fade. The proposal makes the photo the interface: drag down to leave, with the photo shrinking back to the tile it came from, swipe up for Info, double-tap to zoom, tap for a thin filmstrip of neighbours. The buttons stay for accessibility and discoverability, and Delete and Share keep their rules. It needs no new data; the dismiss animation needs the tapped tile's on-screen rectangle, and falls back to today's fade when that tile has scrolled away or the photo was reached from the filmstrip. On the unfolded Fold the same gestures apply to the photo pane, but Info stays the side panel SPEC already gives it: it opens from its button, never from a swipe, and the filmstrip runs along the bottom of the photo pane beside it.

Accept

The viewer opens without the bar; a single tap shows or hides the bar and a filmstrip of neighbouring photos with the position "N of M", and tapping a filmstrip photo moves there; a double-tap zooms 2× around the tap and again returns to fit, with pinch also zooming and a pan inside the zoomed photo; swiping up opens Info as a bottom sheet over the lifted photo and swiping down on the sheet closes it before the viewer; dragging the photo down moves and shrinks it with the finger and fades the backdrop, releasing past a third of the height closes the viewer onto the grid with the photo settling into its tile, and releasing earlier springs back; the Back, Share, Info and Delete controls and Android Back keep working with TalkBack and VoiceOver actions for each gesture; Delete keeps waiting for the hub; only transform and opacity animate and reduced motion makes every transition instant; on the Fold the Info panel stays a side panel opened only by its button, swiping up does nothing, the filmstrip spans the photo pane and not the panel, and a drag down shrinks the photo toward its tile in the wide grid under the same third-of-the-height rule.

UI-FILES-PREVIEW

See a file before opening it

Desktop

Why. Files are the first thing Arca ever did and still the plainest screen: every row is a generic type icon and a size, and File detail lists metadata, so you open a file in another app just to know which of three similarly named PNGs it is. The proposal makes the list recognisable and adds a quick look. Image and video rows swap the type icon for the row preview already used by photo versions (44 px, a still for video); Space on a focused row opens a Quick Look overlay with Previous/Next on the arrow keys and Esc or Space to close; File detail gets a preview header above its stats. Quick Look shows an image or the first frame of a video, the first lines of text and Markdown, an audio file with a play button, and for everything else the type icon with its metadata. A replica holds complete local files, so every preview is generated on the device from the local copy and cached beside the local metadata; the web client served by a hub that is not a replica has no local file, so it would need a server-side preview derivative (the gallery already makes thumbnails and large previews on the hub) and falls back to the icon until one exists. Audio plays through the existing music player, never a second player. Nothing is uploaded and nothing new leaves the device.

Accept

Files rows for images and videos show the 44 px row preview (the same component as photo versions, a still frame for video, the type icon while it loads or when it fails) and every other row keeps its type icon in the same column, so names never shift. Space on a focused row, or Quick Look in the row's menu, opens the overlay; Left and Right move through the folder's files without leaving it, Space or Esc closes and returns focus to the row, Enter or Open runs Open file. Images and video show a fitted preview (video plays only after a click), text, Markdown and code show the first lines in Fragment Mono, audio shows its cover and a play button wired to the music player, and any other type shows the icon, name and metadata with no error. File detail shows the same preview above its stats for previewable types. Previews come from the local copy and are cached with the local metadata, never fetched from the hub for a file this device does not hold; on a client without the file, previewable rows keep the icon. The overlay uses the standard dialog entrance, dismisses immediately, and uses tokens only; reduced motion removes movement.

File detail shows the same preview above its stats for previewable types. Previews are made from the local copy and cached with the local metadata, never fetched from the hub for a file this phone does not hold, and a cache failure leaves the icon. Sheets use the standard entrance, Back and Close dismiss immediately, only transform and opacity animate and reduced motion removes movement; light, dark and 392 dp layouts do not clip.

UI-COMMAND-PALETTE

Find anything with Command-K

Desktop

Why. Arca now holds files, photos, music and history, and each has its own search tucked inside its own folder: to find a track you open Folders, then the music folder, then the search icon. The proposal adds one palette, opened with Command-K or Control-K (and a search field at the top of the sidebar) on desktop and web: a single field, scope chips (All, Files, Photos, Music), results grouped Folders, Files, Photos and Music, and an Actions group for the few commands that matter (Sync now, Pause sync, Choose folders…, Open Settings). With an empty field it lists recent searches and the actions, so it doubles as a launcher. It searches only what this device already holds. The file search per folder and the music index exist today; a cross-folder index of file names and photo file names (no content search, no faces, no places) is the new data, built from the local catalog the replica already keeps.

Accept

Command-K (Control-K on Windows, Linux and web) and a Search field at the top of the sidebar open one dialog from any page; Esc, a click outside or the close tag dismisses it and returns focus to where it was. The field filters as you type with the scope chips All, Files, Photos and Music; results group Folders, Files, Photos (a thumbnail row), Music (track, album, artist with covers) and, for a short action name, Actions, each group capped at a few rows with its count. Up/Down move through rows across groups, Enter opens the folder, file detail, photo viewer or album and plays nothing by itself, and an action runs and closes. An empty field shows recent searches and Actions; a query with no result shows the empty state. It searches this device's local copies only and says so in its footer; the dialog uses the standard dialog entrance (200 ms, no animation on dismiss), tokens only, and reduced motion removes the movement.

UI-MOB-SEARCH

Search everything from the phone and the Fold

Phone · Fold

Why. The phone and the Fold counterpart of UI-COMMAND-PALETTE. Today the phone searches one place at a time: a Files search inside each folder and a library search inside each music folder, so finding a track, a photo and a note means three trips. A phone has no Command-K, so the entry point is a visible Search field at the top of Folders (and of the Fold's page) that opens one full-screen search with the same scope chips, grouping and actions as the desktop palette. It shares the desktop's index of file names, photo names and the music catalog, all read from the device's own complete copies; nothing is sent anywhere, and a cross-folder file-name index is the one thing that does not exist yet. Differences from desktop: no hover or keyboard hints, 48 dp rows and chips, the results sit above the keyboard, and Cancel or the system Back closes it. On the Fold it is a centred two-pane dialog, like the sheets that already become dialogs there, so the rail and the page stay visible behind it and a result can be previewed before leaving the page.

Accept

Phone: Folders opens with a Search Arca field above the first section; tapping it, or the Folders header's search action once the list is scrolled, opens a full-screen search with the keyboard up, a Cancel link and a clear button. The scope chips All, Files, Photos and Music filter as you type; results group Folders, Files, Photos (a four-cell thumbnail row) and Music (tracks with covers), each with its count and capped, with a Show all row per group; a tap opens the folder, file detail, photo viewer or album and plays nothing by itself. An empty field lists the recent searches, each removable with an x, and the actions Sync now, Pause sync, Choose folders… and Open Settings; a query with no result says what search covers. Rows and chips are 48 dp, the results stay above the keyboard and the system Back or Cancel closes search and restores the scroll position. Fold: the same field opens a centred dialog of at most 680 dp over the page, results on the left and the selected result's preview, location and Open photo / Show in folder (Play for a track) on the right, the first result selected; a hardware keyboard's Up/Down moves the selection and Esc closes. Search reads only the device's own copies and index (it works offline), never the hub, and never loads a result's media from the network. Light and dark, accessibility font scaling and 390 dp widths do not clip, and reduced motion removes the entrance.

UI-HISTORY-ACTIVITY

History shows when and where things changed

Desktop

Why. History is an endless list: you cannot see at a glance whether anything happened this week, which machine did it, or what changed while you were away. A 30-day activity strip answers the first question and jumps to a day, each day header says how many changes came from which machine, and one quiet banner on return says what moved while the window was closed. It uses only revisions the hub already keeps; it needs each revision's author device, which the hub's history answer does not expose today. Restoring a whole folder to a day is a natural follow-up and is not proposed here.

Accept

History opens with a Last 30 days strip above the day groups: one bar per day, today at the right, height by number of changes (zero days a hairline), a dot in the conflict or deleted colour above days that have one, a tooltip with the date and counts, and a click or Enter that scrolls to that day's group, loading older pages when needed. Every day header ends in "N changes" with a count per device that made them, using the machine's icon. A banner "Changed while you were away" appears when the window returns to the foreground after at least four hours and something changed meanwhile, lists the count per device, Show scrolls to the first new row and the close button hides it until the next change. The strip and the banner appear identically on the phone's History tab; the strip is a keyboard-reachable group of buttons; reduced motion shows no scroll animation or highlight fade; all colours come from tokens in light and dark; a replica without author data hides the per-device parts and keeps the rest.

UI-MOB-HISTORY-ACTIVITY

History shows when and where things changed on the phone

Phone · Fold

Why. The phone and Fold counterpart of UI-HISTORY-ACTIVITY. The phone's History is the same endless list of day groups, and the phone is the device that is most often away from the others, so the banner for what changed meanwhile matters most here. The strip, the per-day device summary and the banner are the desktop ideas with touch adjustments: the strip is a single 56 dp control rather than thirty targets (a day bar is about 11 dp wide), operated by touch and hold to read a day and a tap or lift to jump, and exposed to TalkBack and VoiceOver as one adjustable slider that steps through days; hover tooltips become the hold chip. Each day header carries its count and the devices behind it, so it needs each revision's author device, which the hub's history does not expose today (the same gap as the desktop board). On the Fold the strip spans the top of the page and History becomes a list beside a revision detail, the two-column pattern folder and file details already use with the 320 dp side column. Restoring a whole folder to a day is a follow-up, not proposed here.

Accept

Phone: History opens with, under the filters, a Last 30 days strip of 30 bars, today at the right, height by number of changes (a day with none is a hairline), a dot in the conflict or deleted colour above days that have one, and an axis of the first day, the middle and Today. The strip is one control at least 48 dp tall: touch and hold shows a chip with the day and its count, dragging scrubs across days, lifting or a tap jumps the list to that day's group, loading older pages when needed; for TalkBack and VoiceOver it is one adjustable slider reading "Sep 27, 1 change". Every day header shows its count and the devices that made the changes, with their icons and names. The banner "Changed while you were away" appears when Arca returns to the foreground after at least four hours and something changed meanwhile, shows the count per device and a dismiss button of at least 48 dp, and stays hidden until the next change. Fold: the strip runs across the top of the page; below it the day groups sit on the left and the selected revision on the right (file, folder, author device, time, revision and File history, which opens the file's history as today), with the first row selected and the banner heading the left list; below 1100 dp the Fold keeps the phone layout. Author device needs the hub to report it per revision. Only opacity and transform animate (the chip fades, the selected day eases in), reduced motion makes both instant, and light, dark and 390 dp widths do not clip.

UI-MOB-NOW-PLAYING

Now playing becomes a page the cover opens into

Phone · Fold

Why. On Android the player is the part of Arca people look at longest, yet Now playing is a plain sheet: a boxed cover, a thin bar and five icons, with no way to reach the artist and no hint of what comes next. The proposal opens it as a full page: the mini player's cover grows into a large one while the page rises, the whole page takes a soft tint of the cover, the artist and album names open their own screens, and Up next peeks at the bottom and lifts into the queue. All of it comes from data the phone already holds (the queue and the cover files); the tint is the one new runtime decision, because it derives a colour from each cover instead of drawing from the palette. iPhone has no player, so this board is Android only. On the unfolded Fold the page is two panes: the cover and controls on the left and Up next always open on the right, so the queue never needs lifting.

Accept

Android only. Tapping the mini player or its cover opens Now playing as a full page: the cover moves and scales from the mini player to the large cover and the page slides up over 200 ms using only transform and opacity, and collapses the same way from the chevron, a downward swipe or Back; reduced motion makes both instant. The page shows Playing from and the album or playlist name, the large cover, the title, the artist and the album as links that open their screens in this folder's library, a scrubber that seeks by touch and by accessibility actions with m:ss times, shuffle, previous, play or pause, next and repeat, and an Up next row that lifts into the queue (the current track marked, the next ones in order, tapping jumps to one). A track still downloading never appears in Up next. The page background is a tint of the playing cover, light at the top and the plain paper by the controls, and the title, controls and times keep at least 4.5:1 contrast on it in light and dark; the tint is a runtime colour derived from the cover and needs the maintainer's validation before building, falling back to the accent tint when a cover is missing. On the Fold the page replaces the centred dialog and fills the window, sidebar included, as two panes: cover, linked names, scrubber and transport on the left and the Up next list on the right at the shared detail side width, with the current track marked and a tap jumping to a track; the open and close transitions use the same transform and opacity rules, the tint and the Android-only scope are unchanged, and a window folded below the sidebar threshold shows the phone page. The iPhone is unchanged.

UI-MOTION-SYSTEM

Motion that shows where things come from

System

Why. Arca is calm, but only route changes, dialogs and notices move, so opening a photo, entering a folder, loading a long list or watching a file arrive all read as cuts. The proposal gives motion one job, showing where a thing came from: a photo grows out of its thumbnail and returns to it, a folder's tile travels into the detail header, the first six rows of a list rise in on a 20 ms stagger, a file that arrives while you watch rises in and its tint settles, and a determinate sync fills a ring around the tile. Everything is opacity and transform and reduced motion collapses it to today's cuts. It needs new tokens and bends three SPEC rules: no decorative animation on polls or row updates (the arrival tint is the one exception), the viewer fades only on opening without scale, and closed dialogs never linger (the viewer stays open for its 280 ms return). It needs no new data; the phone would mirror the tokens later.

Accept

New tokens, mirrored in design-tokens.js and zeroed under reduced motion: --motion-shared 280 ms (photo and folder tile), --motion-stagger 20 ms (at most six rows) and --motion-settle 1,800 ms (arrival tint). The viewer opens from the thumbnail's rectangle and closes back to it, falling back to today's fade when the thumbnail is off screen. A folder card's tile travels to the detail tile. Rows cascade only on route entry, never on refreshes or polls. A row added by sync while its list is visible rises 8 px once and its tint fades; other rows move at once. The determinate ring is built from two rotated half masks, never an animated stroke or size. SPEC Motion and Desktop gallery change with it; a test asserts reduced motion zeroes every new token and that no new keyframe animates a layout property.

UI-MOB-MOTION-SYSTEM

Motion on the phone and the Fold

Phone · Fold

Why. On the phone and the Fold motion is a 30 dp push, sheets that slide or fade-scale and a viewer that fades, so opening a photo, a folder or the player reads as a cut and a touch only tints. This is the counterpart of UI-MOTION-SYSTEM with the same job, showing where a thing came from, plus the touch moments: the cell grows into the viewer, the folder tile travels into the header during the push, the mini player's cover grows into Now playing, a pressed card scales to .98, an edge swipe moves the screen with the finger and a pull on Folders draws the arch mark and starts a cycle. The Fold keeps its rail and single column (list and detail replace each other beside the rail), so it gets the shared elements, a tint that slides down the rail and a dialog that grows from the mini cover. Only transform and opacity animate. It bends SPEC's Mobile and Fold motion rules: tabs no longer only cross-fade, the viewer no longer keeps only its fade, Now playing no longer only slides, row updates get the one arrival exception of the desktop board, and Folders gains a pull gesture and an edge swipe that need native predictive-back support.

Accept

Phone and Fold share new tokens mirrored in design-tokens.js and zeroed under reduced motion: motion-shared 280 ms (cell to viewer, folder tile, player cover), motion-stagger 20 ms (at most six rows), motion-settle 1,800 ms (arrival tint), motion-press-scale .98 with motion-fast 120 ms, and motion-pull-armed 56 dp. Opening a photo animates from the tapped cell's measured rectangle and Back and the drag-down of UI-MOB-VIEWER-GESTURES return to it, falling back to today's fade when the cell is off screen; a folder row's tile travels to the header tile inside the existing 30 dp push; the mini player's cover grows into Now playing's cover on phones and into the dialog's cover on the Fold. Rows cascade only on first entry of a route, never on refreshes or polls, and a row added by sync while its list is visible rises 8 dp once and its tint fades. Cards, rows and buttons scale to .98 while pressed and release in 120 ms. The selected tab tint (phone) and rail tint (Fold) slide in 120 ms while content cross-fades. An edge swipe from the left moves the screen with the finger and completes past a third; Android Back and the Back button keep today's push from the left; this needs predictive back enabled in the native build and a device check. Pulling Folders down past 56 dp starts the same cycle as Sync now: the arch outline draws with the pull, rings while syncing and settles filled; Sync now stays. A determinate cycle fills a ring around the tile built from two rotated half masks, never an animated dimension. Every transition is opacity or transform, reduced motion removes all of them, and light, dark and 760 px layouts do not clip. SPEC's Motion section (Mobile and Fold bullet) is rewritten and design-tokens.js and tokens.css gain the tokens.

UI-BRAND-ARCH

The arch as Arca's visual language

System

Why. The mark is an arch with a door, and the app barely uses it: empty states are a dashed box with one muted glyph, a cover without art shows a note, and "Up to date" is a check mark, so every surface looks like the same utility screen. The proposal reuses the mark's own geometry as a quiet motif: one arch outline for every empty state with the context's glyph in its doorway, an arch silhouette for covers without art, an arch hero on the welcome page, and a status seal whose door fills in when sync settles. It is static line art in existing tokens (tint, green, line and the mark's own door colour), with no new logo and no new illustration style. It needs no new data. Gallery placeholders keep the media glyph SPEC requires.

Accept

Every empty state on desktop, web and phone draws the arch with its current glyph in the doorway; titles, texts and actions stay and the dashed border goes. Music covers and album tiles without art show the arch silhouette; gallery placeholders keep the image and play glyphs. The sidebar and phone status show the seal: outline while syncing or offline, door filled when up to date, door in the warning colour for conflicts. The welcome page opens on the arch hero. The arch is one shared inline SVG from the mark's path, aria-hidden, drawn with existing tokens only and identical in light and dark; a seal change is a 200 ms opacity change, none under reduced motion. SPEC's Placeholders and Foundations bullets are updated.

UI-MOB-BRAND-ARCH

The arch on the phone and the Fold

Phone · Fold

Why. UI-BRAND-ARCH sets the arch as Arca's visual language and draws one phone empty state. The phone has more places for it than desktop: the welcome page opens on a 58 px logo, five empty states are a dashed box with one muted glyph, the Status cell of a folder says "Up to date" in plain text, the splash is a static mark, and albums without art show a note on grey, on the phone and on Android Auto. The proposal reuses the mark's own geometry: an arch hero on the welcome page, the same arch with each screen's glyph in its doorway for empty states, a seal in the Status cell whose door fills when the folder settles, a splash that draws the arch in while the app initialises, and the arch silhouette as the cover fallback (on Android Auto it keeps the green art tile and draws the arch in white and lime for legibility at a glance). The Fold gets the centred welcome flow and the explorer's empty state. No new data, no new logo; the launcher, themed and notification icons are unchanged and shown only so the silhouette reads as the same shape. Gallery placeholders keep the image and play glyphs SPEC requires.

Accept

Every phone and Fold empty state (Folders, Files, Recent, History, a file's history, Devices, the photo grid and the music library) draws the arch with its current glyph in the doorway, keeps its title, text and Retry, and loses the dashed frame; prose prompts keep their text. The folder Status cell shows a seal beside its words: outline while syncing or offline, door filled when up to date, door and edge in the warning colour for a conflict; the word stays, so colour never carries the meaning alone. The splash shows the arch drawn in three layers cross-fading in 600 ms (outline, filled, door lit), held through initialisation as today, and a static final mark under reduced motion; dark paper keeps the cream arch and lime door. Albums and tracks without art show the arch silhouette on the placeholder token on the phone; on Android Auto and in the media notification the green art tile stays and the arch replaces its glyph, in white with the lime door; gallery tiles keep the image and play glyphs. The launcher, themed and notification icons do not change. The arch is one shared SVG from the mark's path, aria-hidden, drawn with existing tokens and identical in light and dark. SPEC's EmptyState primitive, Mobile screens, Launcher and splash and Placeholders bullets are updated and the native build is needed for the splash and the Android Auto cover.

Format 02

Board

One article per idea, tagged with its task ID. It carries the ID, the area (System, Desktop, Mobile, Auto), a title, why it is worth doing, a "Now" drawing of what exists, a "Proposed" drawing, and what proves it done. A mobile board adds a Fold pair beside its phone pair.

Drawings

Both drawings use production and kit classes only, so the page reads as the app would. A drawing is never a screenshot and never introduces a colour or size outside the tokens.

Lifecycle

Idea: a board only. Approved: one ROADMAP Queue line of type ui with the board ID, whose accept is the board. Shipped: the board is deleted, the changelog and SPEC record it, and this kit is updated.

No visual part

Behaviour, data and infrastructure tasks live only in ROADMAP and never get a board.
