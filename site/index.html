01 / One hub. Four libraries.

Everything you keep.
One home.

A private, self-hosted cloud on your own hardware. Files sync to the devices you choose; photos, music and movies open in their own library, with covers, posters and a play button instead of a list of files.

Files.

Folders that sync both ways and work offline. Edit on your laptop, open it on your phone, and go back to an earlier version when you need it.

Photos.

Link a phone album and new photos and videos upload at full quality. Browse them by date on your phone, your computer, the web and your TV.

Music.

Artists, albums, playlists and podcasts from your own files. Each device plays its own copy, offline too, and Android Auto plays it in the car from your phone.

Movies & TV.

Posters, ratings and a Watch next row that follows you from laptop to phone to TV. It all streams from your hub; download a title to your phone or laptop for the road.

Continue on another.

Keep working in the apps you already use. Arca carries your changes between devices, so you pick up where you left off without emailing yourself a copy.

Offline is a normal day.

Once a folder finishes syncing, its files are on your device. Open them anywhere; your edits sync when the hub is reachable again.

A way back.

Restore an earlier version from your hub’s history, kept for 30 days by default and adjustable per folder. Restoring needs the hub to be reachable. When two edits collide, Arca keeps both for you to review.

You run it, so you keep it.

Your folders are ordinary files on your own disks, with no Arca account, public relay or telemetry. The trade-off: you look after the hub, its storage and its backups. Its history is kept in Arca’s own format, so Arca is what reads it.

03 / Bring your devices together

One hub.
Your devices around it.

One computer or server is your hub: it keeps your folders, your libraries and their history, and every device you pair talks to it. Leave it on and nothing waits for your laptop to wake up. Phones, tablets and TVs connect to a hub; they cannot be one.

  1. 01

    Pick your hub.

    Install Arca on a computer you can leave on, or run it with Docker on a server or NAS. A setup wizard guides you; a server asks for a one-time code printed by a command you run on it.

  2. 02

    Pair your devices.

    Each device joins with a single-use pairing code from the hub. At home, switch on local network access in the hub’s settings; it is plain HTTP. Away from home, use a private network you control, such as Tailscale.

  3. 03

    Choose your folders.

    Each device picks whole folders on its own. On a computer you choose where they go; on a phone, Arca keeps them in app storage.

  4. 04

    Set up your libraries.

    On the hub, choose Set up as library… on a folder and pick Photos, Audio or Movies & TV. A phone that links an album fills a gallery and keeps the complete shared folder in app storage; Arca never imports into your phone’s Photos. For posters and details, add your own free TMDB key in Settings.

  5. 05

    Check how long history is kept.

    Each synced folder keeps earlier versions for 30 days by default; change it per folder to Off, 1 day, 1 week, 30 days or Forever. A shorter window removes older versions for good. Movies & TV keeps no history.

Questions, answered

Before you get started.

Is Arca really free?

Yes. There is no price, no account and no subscription. It is free for personal and non-commercial use under the PolyForm Strict License, so using it for a business or at work is not covered; you can read the source, but it is not open source.

Do I need a server to use Arca?

A dedicated server is optional. A computer you already own can be the hub, or you can run one with Docker. Phones and tablets connect to a hub; they cannot act as one.

What happens if my hub breaks?

Your devices keep complete copies of the folders they chose. Folders no device chose and the history live only on the hub, unless you turned on the optional full backup on a desktop or server. Movies & TV stays only on the hub: it keeps no history and the backup skips it, so keep your own copy of your video files. A desktop or server with a complete copy of every shared folder can take over as the new hub, with a fresh history.

Is sync a backup?

Sync also carries file deletions between ordinary folder copies. Retained history offers a way back, but keep an independent backup. Arca’s optional full hub backup is off by default and runs on a desktop or server, separately from working copies; it does not include Movies & TV.

Who can see my data?

Only you and the devices you pair: there is no company server in the middle and no telemetry. If you add a TMDB key, the hub sends the titles and years of your movies and shows to TMDB for posters and details. The desktop app checks GitHub for signed updates, and Open in Maps on a photo opens Google Maps. Arca is not end-to-end encrypted: paired devices are trusted with your files and a local network without HTTPS carries plain HTTP, so if you cannot trust your network, use a private network such as Tailscale.

Where do my files live?

On your hub and on the devices that sync them, as ordinary files any app can open: desktop copies in folders you choose, phone copies in app storage. The hub’s history and optional backup use Arca’s own format. Your own disks set the capacity.

Does every device need to keep everything?

No. Each device chooses whole folders independently, and the folders you sync download as complete files, ready for offline use. Movies & TV stays on the hub and streams; download a single title when you want it offline. A linked phone album adds its photos to a shared folder, and that phone then keeps the complete folder in app storage like any other device.

Does my hub have to stay on?

To exchange changes and to stream. Once a folder finishes syncing, its local files work offline, and edits wait until the hub is reachable again. Movies & TV and everything on arca TV stream from the hub, so they need it, except a title you downloaded beforehand. An always-on hub keeps all of it within reach.

Can I use Arca away from home?

Yes, if your device can reach the hub. Arca has no public relay, so away from home you need a private network you control, such as Tailscale (a separate service you set up yourself), or your own HTTPS address.

How is Arca different from Dropbox, Nextcloud or Syncthing?

Dropbox, iCloud and Google Drive keep your files on a company’s servers; Nextcloud is a self-hosted platform for many people; Syncthing syncs device to device, with no hub. Arca keeps complete copies of the folders each device chooses, around a hub you run that keeps their history. It has no accounts or per-person permissions, so it suits one person or a group that trusts each other; for per-person sharing or no setup at all, those tools may suit you better.

Can Arca replace Google Photos, iCloud or Plex?

For one person, or a household that trusts each other, it covers the same ground on your own hardware: photo and video uploads from your phone, a music and podcast library, and movies and shows with posters and Watch next on your laptop, phone and TV. It has no face recognition or AI search, no sharing links for people outside your devices, and it never re-encodes video: a file whose audio a device cannot play gets a compatible audio track.

How do photo albums work?

Link a phone album to a hub folder. Arca uploads new photos and videos without recompressing the originals; browse them by date on desktop or the web. Deleting from your phone’s gallery keeps the uploaded files, and hub changes never write back to your Photos library. Deleting a photo in Arca’s shared gallery removes it from every device’s Arca copy, and it stays recoverable while history is kept.

How does music work?

Choose Set up as library… → Audio on a hub folder. Every device that keeps it browses artists, albums, playlists and recent plays and plays its own complete copy, offline too, on desktop, the web, Android and Android Auto; iPhone browses it and opens tracks in other apps for now. The playlists you make are saved as ordinary files in that folder. An Android app installed outside the Play Store needs Unknown sources turned on in Android Auto’s developer settings.

How do Movies & TV work?

Choose Set up as library… → Movies & TV on a hub folder. The hub names movies and shows from their files, and with your own free TMDB key adds posters, overviews, ratings and cast. Every device streams from the hub without re-encoding the video, picking up where you left off on another; the phone and the desktop app can download a title to watch offline, and arca TV plays it on your television.

Will my phone sync in the background?

Background work follows iOS and Android scheduling limits. Arca also resumes work when you open it. Keep the app open for a large first upload; background sync is not continuous.

Can I undo an edit or recover a deleted file?

Yes, for as long as the folder’s history keeps it: 30 days by default, adjustable per folder. Restoring needs the hub to be reachable and adds a new version, leaving earlier history intact. Conflicting edits are kept as separate versions for you to review.

How do I update Arca?

The desktop app updates itself from signed releases. Update a Docker server by pulling the new image; Android updates through Google Play. Check the release notes for any requirement to update the hub and its devices together.
